# Engineering Without a Starting Point - Decision-Making Inside an Existing System

## There Is No Starting Point

There's a moment in a career when you realize
that real engineering almost never starts.

There's no blank screen.
No clean diagram.
No "let's do this right from the beginning."

There's an existing system.

With decisions from the past.
With code written for reasons that are no longer relevant.
With constraints no one chose - but everyone lives with.

And the question is no longer
how would we design an ideal system,
but how do you make decisions inside what already exists.

## The Illusion: "If Only We Started Over"

Almost every mature system carries familiar phrases:

- "This is temporary"
- "We'll refactor it later"
- "This isn't how we'd do it today"

And the most dangerous sentence of all:
"if we started now - it would look different."

But we're not starting now.
And the system doesn't wait.

It's running.
Users depend on it.
Changes are happening around it and inside it.

## Engineering Without Privileges

Engineering with a starting point is a luxury.

Engineering without a starting point means:

- Decisions get made with partial information
- Every change affects something else
- There's no "correct" solution - only different risks

This isn't a weakness of the system.
It's its natural state.

## Why This Series Exists

This series isn't about
how to design a system.

And it isn't about
how to build a "beautiful" architecture.

It's about entirely different questions:

- How do you decide when there's no time
- How do you change things without breaking everything
- How do you lead without erasing the past
- And how do you know when not to touch something

This is a series about engineering as an ongoing activity -
not a one-time event.

## The Real Starting Point

The series' starting point is simple:

The system is already here.
With everything good about it.
And everything that gets in the way.

And our job isn't
to fix the past,
but to choose how to live with its consequences.
